# A language built through action

The collection should make abstraction feel familiar before it needs a name. Its aspiration is agency: a learner can alter a system, discover a relationship, and carry that relationship somewhere new.

The recurring pattern is **identity through change**. A disc keeps its partner as the arrangement unfolds. An object keeps its properties while moving between proposed groupings. A shape keeps its identity while passing through a routing mechanism. Eventually, an entire mechanism becomes something the learner can pick up and compose.

This is the series' organizing idea. The material can become more powerful without requiring a new visual vocabulary on every screen.

## The first shared vocabulary

| Visual role | Treatment in this prototype | What it carries forward |
|---|---|---|
| An object | A small, precise shape with a warm material and a large touch target | Persistent identity |
| A possible destination | An open ring or a region with a stable boundary | A place in a proposed construction |
| A relationship | A fine line attached to its endpoints | Correspondence and dependence |
| Two simultaneous rules | Overlapping translucent boundaries with examples at their edges | Shared membership without duplication |
| An operation | A translucent routing module with visible internal paths | A transformation that can itself become an object |
| Inspection | A shared scrubber and a bounded play gesture | Control over the explanation's pace |
| An event | A brief local ring of light and optional quiet tone | An observable consequence |

Warm objects, cool structure, thin connections, rounded destinations, and generous space recur throughout the collection. Color is reinforced by geometry. The subtle glow belongs to interaction and selected relationships. There are no decorative particle counts competing with the mathematical objects.

Every experience uses native HTML controls with nonvisual accessible names. The learning surface has no written words or numerals. Written explanations are confined to supporting documents and assistive descriptions.

## Grow through recombination

The first lesson introduces a relationship between objects. The second lets the same object participate in two relationships. The third lets relationships act in succession, then turns the transformations into movable objects.

Later experiences can reuse these ideas:

1. A routing mechanism becomes a permutation matrix when its paths are recorded in cells.
2. A deterministic route becomes a probabilistic branch when several outcomes carry weights.
3. Repeated branching builds path multiplicities and random walks.
4. Combining shifted contributions leads into convolution and polynomial multiplication.
5. A modulus relation redirects contributions into a finite collection of coordinates.
6. Repeated small changes lead into accumulation, approximation, and eventually limits under explicitly developed assumptions.

These are connections to investigate. They are not a claim that a single screen communicates all of their formal content.

## Principles used in the implementation

**The gesture has a consequence.** Direct manipulation immediately affects the visible arrangement. Proximity cues appear near a possible connection; they do not replace the action with a score.

**The identity survives.** Rearrangement retains the same objects and links. Sorting retains one copy of each token. Routing retains the same three shapes, even when order changes their destinations.

**Time belongs to the learner.** Demonstrations are bounded and pausable. Scrubbing evaluates an inspectable state. Motion preferences are respected. An interrupted drag restores the prior arrangement.

**A contradiction remains visible.** A ring can remain empty, an extra object can remain unmatched, and a proposed classification can disagree with the fixed rules. These states support further action.

**Composition is the path to complexity.** The first screens remain small. New complexity arrives by connecting familiar actions, rather than by adding an unrelated dashboard of controls.

**A good experience can outgrow its example.** A next example changes positions, shapes, counts, or the particular permutation. Supporting physical tasks can test whether the relationship travels beyond the original display.

## What needs observation

The wordless controls are design proposals. Their meanings must become learnable through experience; an attractive icon alone does not establish that a child understands it.

Observe where a learner discovers an affordance, imitates a movement, anticipates an outcome, and reconstructs the relationship in a fresh case. Keep those observations distinct. Familiarity with the controls, motor skill, and mathematical understanding can each affect behavior.

The first collection has not yet undergone learner observation. Its purpose is to make the design concrete enough to assess and improve.
